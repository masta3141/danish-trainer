# Word data

`words.js` and `forms.json` are generated by `tools/build-words.py` — do not edit them by hand.

## Sources and licenses

- **Words, word class, frequency order:** [De hyppigste ord i dansk](https://korpus.dsl.dk/resources/details/freq-lemmas.html)
  (30,000 most frequent lemmas, ~880 million words of text 1983–2016), Det Danske Sprog- og Litteraturselskab (DSL),
  prepared by Jørg Asmussen. License: [DSL Åben Licens](https://korpus.dsl.dk/resources/licences/dsl-open.html).
  The app uses the 5000 most frequent words after removing proper nouns, numerals, compound fragments and corpus noise.
- **Levels A1–C1:** not from any official list — the DSL list has no CEFR levels. They are frequency bands:
  A1 = words 1–500, A2 = 501–1000, B1 = 1001–2000, B2 = 2001–3500, C1 = 3501–5000.
- **Gender (en/et), inflected forms (forms line and `forms.json`):** English Wiktionary via the
  [kaikki.org](https://kaikki.org/dictionary/Danish/) extract. License: **CC BY-SA**. Nouns without an inflection
  table in Wiktionary get regular forms in `forms.json` (sentence recognition only).
- **German glosses:** hand-written for this app (`tools/glosses-de.tsv`), using the English Wiktionary glosses only
  to tell meanings apart.

## Known quirks

- The DSL corpus is mostly newspaper and fiction text, so politics and news vocabulary rank high
  (e.g. *regering*, *kommune* in A1) and some everyday words come later than a textbook would have them.
- A few DSL lemmas are not the forms learners use; the display form replaces them (*megen* → *meget*,
  *mangen* → *mange*, *syne* → *synes*, *går* → *i går*, *fjor* → *i fjor*).
